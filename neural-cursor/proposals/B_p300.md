# Proposal B — P300 / oddball ERP selection mapped onto cursor movement

## 1. Paradigm and signal basis

**Signal.** A ring of 8 direction discs (plus a central "stop/commit" disc) is drawn around the cursor and rides with it. Discs are intensified one at a time in random permutations (stimulus 100 ms on, SOA 150–175 ms). When the attended disc flashes, the EEG shows the oddball complex: P300/P3b over Cz–Pz (250–500 ms), plus N1/P1 and N2 visual components over PO7/PO8/Oz that are large only when the disc is foveated. Every flash is a labelled event (attended vs not attended, 1:7 prior), and per-item evidence is accumulated across repetitions until a Bayesian stopping rule commits a direction. The cursor can move in discrete steps per commit or, in the quasi-continuous variant, with a velocity proportional to the running posterior after every flash (Citi, Poli, Cinel & Sepulveda 2008, IEEE TNSRE, "P300-based BCI mouse with genetically optimised analogue control"; Salvaris, Cinel, Citi & Poli 2010, IEEE TNSRE).

**Why it is not "natural", stated up front.** This paradigm requires (a) external visual stimulation and (b) overt attention: the user must look at and count/attend the disc they want. Treder & Blankertz (2010, Behav. Brain Funct.) and Brunner et al. (2010, J. Neural Eng., "Does the 'P300' speller depend on eye gaze?") both showed that when gaze is withdrawn (covert attention) ERP-speller accuracy collapses to roughly half or less of the overt value, because the early visual components carry much of the discriminative information. So on this hardware the decoder is, honestly, a *gaze-plus-attention* detector. It violates the user's two stated preferences (no flicker/oddball) in letter, not just in spirit. What it does offer is the most *estimable* label structure of any EEG paradigm (Section 4) and the most mature, dry-electrode-validated stack on g.tec hardware (intendiX runs this on g.Nautilus/g.SAHARA).

**Least intrusive variant.** (i) Low-contrast (≈15–25 % Michelson) luminance or apparent-motion onsets instead of white flashes (motion-onset "N200 speller", Hong et al. 2009, Clin. Neurophysiol.; Jin et al. 2012) — lower annoyance and habituation, modest accuracy cost; (ii) stimulation only while the cursor is "armed" (user dwells on the ring), silent otherwise; (iii) tactile or spatial-auditory oddballs (Brouwer & van Erp 2010, Front. Neurosci.; Schreuder, Blankertz & Tangermann 2010, PLoS ONE) remove the screen flicker entirely but roughly halve ITR. None of these makes it stimulus-free.

## 2. Expected performance on this hardware

Anchor literature (gel electrodes unless stated):

| Krusienski et al. 2006, J. Neural Eng.; 2008, J. Neurosci. Methods | classifier comparison; 8-channel set Fz Cz Pz P3 P4 PO7 PO8 Oz | SWLDA/FLD best; ≈90 % accuracy at 15 sequences; 8 channels suffice |

| Barachant & Congedo 2014 (arXiv) ; Rivet et al. 2009, IEEE TBME | xDAWN + Riemannian "plug & play" P300 | cross-subject classifier usable without calibration, single-flash AUC ≈0.75–0.85 |

**Translation to 8-direction cursor steering.** Bits per committed selection at 8 classes: 2.25 bits at 90 % accuracy, 1.60 bits at 80 %. With gel, dynamic stopping typically commits after 2–5 repetitions: 8 items × 3.5 rep × 0.16 s ≈ 4.5 s + 0.8 s epoch tail ≈ 5.3 s → ≈0.40 bits/s (24 bits/min), consistent with Townsend's 23 bits/min.

**Dry-electrode penalty.** The g.SAHARA data (Guger 2012) say the *ceiling* survives, but with 32 pin electrodes through hair at Pz/PO7/PO8/Oz, I budget a single-flash AUC drop of 0.05–0.08 (≈0.80 → 0.72–0.75), which the posterior-stopping rule converts into ≈1.5× more repetitions. Realistic dry range:

- selection accuracy 80–90 % (10th-percentile user ≈65 %);
- 6–8 s per committed direction → **0.20–0.35 bits/s** (12–21 bits/min);
- Fitts throughput for cursor-to-target (two-level zoom: region then sub-region, 6 bits in 2 commits ≈14 s): **≈0.25–0.40 bits/s**; stepping mode ≈0.15–0.25 bits/s;
- time-to-target for an ID≈3.5-bit screen target: 12–20 s (zoom), 25–45 s (stepping), including ≈10 % error-recovery.

This is roughly 2–4× below a trained 2-D sensorimotor-rhythm user (Wolpaw & McFarland 2004), but available to ≈90 % of naive users within 15 minutes. The velocity mode does not raise the information rate; it only hides the discreteness.

## 3. Scaling behaviour (bitter lesson)

**What more data buys.**
1. *Cross-subject prior (hours × subjects).* A Riemannian/xDAWN or EEGNet-style model pretrained on public ERP corpora (BNCI Horizon 2020 P300 sets; Lee et al. 2019 GigaScience, 54 subjects × 2 sessions; Won et al. 2022; Aricò et al. 2014) gives first-use accuracy ≈80–90 % of individual-calibrated (Barachant & Congedo 2014; Kindermans 2014). Mechanism: ERP topographies are far more consistent across people than sensorimotor rhythms.
2. *Unsupervised data engine (hours × sessions).* Every flash in ordinary use is a labelled sample once the committed direction is accepted (not undone within 3 s). Kindermans' EM self-training plus Riemannian re-centring (Zanini et al. 2018, IEEE TBME) turns use into calibration; expected curve: single-flash AUC +0.03–0.05 over the first ≈300 target epochs (≈15 min), then flat.
3. *Intent prior (sessions).* Analogous to the language model in spellers (Mainsah et al. 2015): a learned prior over next direction given cursor state and goal history lowers repetitions needed by ≈20–30 %. This is the only term that keeps scaling with use, and it is a *policy* term, not a decoding term.
4. *Co-adaptation.* The user learns to foveate and count more reliably; the decoder re-centres per block. Expect +5–10 % accuracy over sessions 1→3, then plateau.

**Where it saturates and why.** Single-trial P300 SNR is fixed by physics (~−10 to −20 dB), so information per flash is bounded (≈0.3–0.5 bits/flash at AUC 0.8); the stimulus rate is bounded (SOA < 125 ms makes overlapping ERPs; Townsend used 125 ms) and attention/habituation lower amplitudes with repeated use. Therefore the scaling curve is steep and short: zero → 15 min of data gets you most of the way; 100 hours gets you perhaps one fewer repetition. Pretraining on a million hours of resting EEG will not raise bits-per-flash much; the bottleneck is evoked-response SNR, not representation learning. Honest ceiling here: ≈0.5 bits/s.

## 4. Data-collection protocol

**Participants.** N = 24 healthy adults (no performance-based exclusion; exclusions only for consent withdrawal or a hardware failure logged before analysis). 3 sessions each: day 1, day 2–7, day 14–30 (held-out session 3).

**Session (≈80 min).**
1. Cap fit, impedance log on all 32 channels, 10 min.
2. *Copy-task calibration* (used only for the supervised baseline; the zero-calibration arm skips it): 40 trials × (2 s cue + 8 discs × 5 reps × 0.16 s) ≈ 5.5 min. Cue = highlighted target disc; label = cue identity.
3. *Fitts cursor task* 4 blocks × 7 min: targets at ID 2, 3, 4, 5 bits (Shannon ID, 2-D), order Latin-squared; the goal is shown to the user but **not** to the stimulus generator or decoder (flash sequence is a pre-generated random permutation stream, seeded per block).
4. *Surrogate blocks* (randomised order): (a) **covert** block — fixate centre dot, attend disc covertly (quantifies gaze dependence); (b) **passive** block — count central dot colour changes while discs flash (flash-locked VEP floor, no intent); (c) **sham-target** block — cue instructs the user to attend disc *k* but labels are assigned to a random disc *k'* (label-permutation floor measured online); (d) FP1/FP2-only decoder run post hoc on all blocks (ocular surrogate), and when the eye tracker is added, gaze-only decoder.
5. Delayed keypress on every commit ("was that the direction you wanted?"), yes/no, to get an intent label independent of the display.

**LSL streams/markers.** EEG 32 ch @ 500 Hz; aux photodiode channel on disc 0 (ground truth for stimulus latency; jitter tolerance 2 ms); marker stream: flash onset (item id, round id, block seed index), target id (when cued), posterior vector at every flash, commit event, keypress, cursor x/y at 60 Hz, impedance snapshot every block, pipeline git hash.

**Gating.** Per channel RMS > 5× session median RMS (FP1/FP2 exempt) → flag epoch; > 20 % flagged channels → discard round, log. Impedance > 500 kΩ on dry pins → flag channel, continue with xDAWN down-weighting.

**Pre-registration list.** Primary endpoints, floors, surrogate decoders, stopping thresholds, pipeline versions, N, analysis code hash, and the falsifiers: (F1) if the FP1/FP2-only decoder's AUC is within 0.05 of the full decoder, the system is declared gaze-driven; (F2) if passive-block AUC ≥ 0.65, the flash-locked VEP alone explains the result; (F3) if session-3 zero-calibration accuracy < 60 %, transfer is declared failed.

**Power.** Co-primary 1 (accuracy vs permutation floor) is over-powered at N = 24 (expected 80 % vs 12.5 %). Co-primary 2 (Fitts throughput, session 3 vs session 1, paired): detectable effect d = 0.6 at 80 % power, α = 0.05. Secondary (dry-vs-gel? not run; zero-calibration vs supervised): TOST with equivalence margin ±5 accuracy points, N = 24 gives ≈80 % power at σ = 8.

## 5. Decoder and system

**Preprocessing (causal, online).** 50 Hz notch; 2nd-order Butterworth 0.5–20 Hz (SOS, forward only); epoch 0–0.8 s post-flash; decimate 500 → 50 Hz (40 samples); no re-referencing beyond g.tec reference; bad-channel mask from gating.

**Features/classifier.** xDAWN (Rivet 2009) with 4 filters per class fitted on target/non-target prototypes; ERP "super-trial" covariance (Barachant & Congedo 2014): stack [filtered prototypes; filtered epoch] → 12×12 SPD matrix; shrinkage (Ledoit–Wolf); tangent-space projection at the Riemannian mean (78 features); logistic regression, L2, C = 1, class_weight balanced. Per flash output log-odds; per item sum log-odds across repetitions → softmax posterior over 9 items with prior from the intent model (uniform at session 1).

**Dynamic stopping.** Commit when max posterior ≥ 0.90 (zoom mode) / ≥ 0.80 (velocity mode), min 2 reps, max 8 reps; else commit to argmax at rep 8 and flag low-confidence.

**Transfer / zero calibration.** Riemannian re-centring of each session's covariances to the identity (reference estimated on first 60 s, updated as EMA α = 0.02); classifier pretrained on pooled public ERP data + prior participants; unsupervised EM over item identity (Kindermans 2012) runs during use. Supervised arm uses copy-task calibration only (baseline).

**Online latency budget.** Flash → epoch end 800 ms; LSL + buffer 20 ms; filtering/xDAWN/TS/LR < 5 ms; decision available ≈830 ms after the last flash of a round. Cursor update tick 60 Hz.

**Cursor dynamics.** Velocity mode: v = g·Σ_i p_i·d_i (d_i unit direction, p_i posterior, stop disc contributes 0), g = 0.08 screen/s at full confidence, EMA smoothing α = 0.3, posterior reset after commit. Zoom mode (hex-o-spell-like, Blankertz et al. 2006 / Treder & Blankertz 2010): commit 1 picks a region (1/8 screen), commit 2 picks a sub-region; cursor jumps to its centre; 3rd commit = click. No target snapping or assistance that depends on the goal (would recreate the RLNF display-criterion leak).

**Co-adaptation rule.** Decoder: re-centre every 60 s; EM update every 20 commits; intent prior updated per session from committed directions. User: on-screen posterior bar so they see confidence growing; no explicit training beyond "look at the disc and count its flashes".

**Stack.** Python 3.11, pylsl, MNE 1.6, pyriemann 0.5, scikit-learn 1.4, PsychoPy 2024 for stimuli with photodiode patch; seeds fixed; all versions pinned; pipeline hash in the marker stream.

## 6. Evaluation protocol

- **Co-primary endpoints.** (1) Online 9-class selection accuracy in the Fitts task (session 3, zero-calibration arm), reported per subject with Wilson CIs and a 95 % prediction interval for a new participant. (2) Fitts throughput (bits/s, Shannon ID, effective width) and time-to-target.
- **Permutation floors at matched n.** 1,000 within-round label shuffles per subject (preserving flash structure and repetitions) → empirical chance for accuracy and for single-flash AUC; the sham-target block gives the same floor measured online.
- **Non-neural surrogates (pre-registered).** FP1/FP2-only decoder; photodiode/flash-timing-only decoder (must be at floor by construction — the flash sequence is independent of the target); gaze-only decoder once an eye tracker is attached; passive-viewing VEP floor.
- **Identifiability argument.** The label (attended item) is set by the cue (calibration) or the delayed keypress (free use), and the only display variable that differs between target and non-target epochs is *which item flashed*, which is randomised independently of the target. Hence no deterministic display mediator exists. The *real* confound is foveation, which is measured (covert block, FP1/FP2 surrogate, future gaze stream) and reported, not hidden.
- **Statistics.** Mixed-effects model (subject random intercept) for accuracy and throughput across sessions; effect sizes with bootstrap CIs; TOST (±5 points) for zero-calibration vs supervised; the held-out set is session 3 for all decoder variants, and 6 pre-assigned participants are held out from any pretraining pool. The test set is fixed before any resource-ladder analysis (0, 5, 15, 30 min of self-training data).
- **Reproducibility.** Deterministic pipeline, hyperparameters above, raw XDF files, analysis notebook keyed to the pipeline hash.

## 7. Failure modes and debugging

1. *Gaze does the work.* Detect via F1/covert block; mitigate with gaze-independent stimuli (motion-onset, tactile) or accept and reframe the P300 ring as a confirmation/"click" channel paired with another controller.
2. *Habituation / fatigue.* P300 amplitude declines within a session; use low-contrast or motion-onset stimuli, 5-min breaks every 20 min, and track amplitude at Pz as a monitored secondary.
3. *Adjacency/double-flash errors* (Fazel-Rezai 2007): never flash neighbouring discs consecutively (checkerboard-style constraint in the permutation generator, Townsend 2010).
4. *Dry-electrode motion/EMG artefacts.* Amplitude gating, xDAWN down-weighting of noisy channels, jaw/blink instructions, and the 5× RMS flags logged per round; if > 30 % rounds are flagged, re-seat the cap.
5. *Stimulus timing jitter* corrupts ERP averaging: photodiode check every session, reject sessions with jitter SD > 3 ms.
6. *Self-training collapse* when accuracy < 65 %: EM updates are confidence-gated (posterior ≥ 0.9 only) and reverted if copy-task-style spot checks drop > 10 points.
7. *Cross-session non-stationarity* (cap placement): re-centring; if session-3 accuracy drops > 15 points, fall back to 3-min supervised calibration.
8. *User dislikes flicker* — the central, unavoidable problem: offer armed-only and tactile variants; if still rejected, use this paradigm only as the click channel in a hybrid.

## 8. Honest self-score

1. **Naturalness: 2/10.** Requires flashing stimuli and overt attention to them; the least intrusive variants reduce annoyance but never remove the stimulation.
2. **Achievable continuous-control performance: 6/10.** Literature-anchored 0.2–0.35 bits/s with dry electrodes, available to ≈90 % of users in minutes, but inherently discrete and 2–4× below trained-MI continuous control.
3. **Scaling with data: 5/10.** Cross-subject transfer and unsupervised self-training genuinely deliver zero-calibration use, but the curve saturates within ≈15 minutes because single-flash SNR and stimulus rate bound the information per unit time.
4. **Validity/identifiability: 9/10.** Randomised flash sequences make a display-criterion leak impossible by construction; the gaze confound is explicit, measured by covert/FP1-FP2/gaze surrogates, and pre-registered as a falsifier.
5. **Feasibility & user burden: 7/10.** Mature, commercially deployed stack on g.tec hardware; dry g.SAHARA validation exists; 0–5 min calibration; cost is flicker tolerance and a photodiode.
6. **Evaluation rigour: 8/10.** Pre-registered co-primary endpoints, matched-n permutation floors, online sham block, held-out session and participants, pinned deterministic pipeline.

Weighted total: (2×25 + 6×20 + 5×20 + 9×15 + 7×10 + 8×10)/100 = **5.55/10**.

## 9. Five-sentence pitch

P300 selection is the only EEG paradigm on this dry 32-channel cap that gives ≈90 % of naive users reliable control within their first fifteen minutes, with no calibration thanks to cross-subject Riemannian transfer and unsupervised self-training. Its labels are generated by a random flash sequence that is independent of the target, so the exact display-criterion leak that sank RLNF cannot occur, and its remaining confound — gaze — is measured rather than hidden. Dynamic stopping and a learned intent prior turn a discrete selector into a quasi-continuous 0.2–0.35 bits/s steering channel that is honest about being 2–4× slower than a trained continuous controller. It fails the user's naturalness criterion outright, and I say so: the strongest honest role for it is as the rigorous, dry-electrode-proven click/commit channel in a hybrid with a stimulus-free controller. If the judge wants a system that works on day one and can be falsified cleanly, this is it; if the judge wants "just naturally move the cursor", it is not.
