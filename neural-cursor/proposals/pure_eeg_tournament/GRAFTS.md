# Grafts and factual errors recorded by the judges, per match

## QF-live_in-vs-population: live_in vs population — winner population (0–3)
- graft: Population should take live_in's recency-controlled decomposition for its depth arm. That means a volume ladder in which every training set ends just before a fixed held-out test set and extends backwards; a control of fixed 8-h windows at increasing lags; and a fresh decoder cross-validated on exactly 300 hidden trials at intervals to track separability. All test data should come from a dedicated evaluation probe stream, never used for selection or ensemble weighting. Population's headline panel (c), the "own-hours equivalent of the prior", is currently an own-data ladder grown forwards in time, so it carries the recency confound live_in was attacked for. Without this graft the panel cannot tell data volume from non-stationarity or from user learning. If a second graft fits, population should also add live_in's goal-blind auditor veto (operant extinction) to its 30-h co-adaptive depth sessions.
- graft: Population should take live_in's split of hidden probes into two interleaved streams. S-probes (selection, ensemble weights, co-adaptive updates and the deployment gate) would be kept separate from E-probes, the evaluation stream of record. E-probes are never used to train, select or tune, and each one is scored prospectively by the model that is live at that moment. Clean windows (gaze within 1 deg, no microsaccade within +/-100 ms) define the headline set. Population needs this because, as amended, it co-adapts its last layer every 60 s on hidden-probe trials and also uses hidden-probe cosine as its closed-loop evidence. That is the same evaluation-leakage problem live_in conceded and fixed. Live_in's goal-blind auditor veto, which freezes the cursor and gives zero adaptation weight when EMG, gaze or the IMU trip, is the second-best graft for the co-adaptive block.
- graft: Live_in's goal-blind auditor veto, together with its split between S-probes (used for selection) and E-probes (the evaluation stream of record). Population's 10-minute co-adaptive blocks and its 4 x 30 h nightly fine-tuned depth arm are exactly where operant shaping of ocular or muscle strategies could occur, and population currently only detects that after the fact. The veto turns detection into extinction while keeping the no-exclusion rule. The S/E split stops the depth arm's deployment gate from being scored on the same hidden-probe test sets that certify the own-hours-equivalent curve.

- strongest objection to live_in: Live_in's headline is 'purely cortical' control certified on hidden probes where 'the screen carries zero information about the goal', but that removes only the display, not the eyes or the muscles. On hidden probes the person still knows a goal ring at 4 deg eccentricity, and the proposal explicitly aims at covert-attention alpha lateralisation to it. Covert attention comes with goal-directed microsaccades (Hafed & Clark 2002; Engbert & Kliegl 2003), and miniature saccades produce EEG transients (Yuval-Greenberg 2008). The decoder keeps FP1/FP2/F7/F8/T7/T8 as inputs. Fixation-break trials are retained. The montage-ablation bar lets 30% of above-chance accuracy come from non-sensorimotor channels. A free-strategy co-adaptive loop, updated every 60 s for 200 h, is an optimiser that the proposal itself says will exploit 'jaw tension, micro-saccades and head motion', and admits 'nothing fully prevents it'. On this project's own ds004362 audit, a frontal eye proxy already beats cortex (0.73 vs 0.60). So the most probable thing a slowly rising per-person curve would measure is a person learning to steer with ocular and cranial-muscle leakage into a dry cap. The two arms meant to separate human from machine (a recency-confounded data ladder and a mismatched frozen-decoder probe) cannot tell the difference, and the same hidden probes that certify the curve also select which models get deployed.
- strongest objection to population: The population proposal's central mechanism, pooling people so the model learns the cross-person invariant part, is refuted or inverted by its own ds004362 evidence. The cortical curve is flat by N≈16-32 (+0.023 from N=8 to 76, not significant). Meanwhile the eye proxy, the signal that really is anatomically invariant across people, rises to 0.73 and dominates a combined model (weights 1.17 vs 0.36). Yet the planned decoder is an 8M-parameter transformer on all 32 channels, including Fp1/Fp2/F7/F8, after common-average referencing, and the closed-loop headline runs with gaze free and flashed targets. The likely headline is therefore either a flat participant curve, an incremental result given zero-training BCI work dating to Krauledat 2008 and Fazli 2009, or a rising curve that its own surrogates must attribute to eyes. The jump to 0.67-0.70 at N=160 is justified by '8x more trials per person', which contradicts the proposal's own finding that the per-person axis is flat.

## QF-brain_reward-vs-generative: brain_reward vs generative — winner generative (1–2)
- graft: A's artefact-referenced topography test should replace B's circular 'must peak frontocentrally at 200-600 ms' test. In A's version, each session records 20 blinks and 20 vertical saccades to map that person's ocular propagation. The decoder's Haufe-2014 forward pattern must correlate |rho| < 0.5 with that map and show the negativity-then-positivity structure at FCz/Cz. Pair it with A's effect-size purity margin: EEG AUC minus the nonlinear union-surrogate AUC must be at least 0.03, and EEG+union minus union at least 0.03, both with CIs excluding 0. This replaces B's 'partial information > 0', which is trivially significant at n of about 10^5 epochs.
- graft: B should adopt A's quantitative purity criteria in place of its weakest tests:
- A nonlinear (gradient-boosted) union surrogate of eye, microsaccade, pupil, EMG, display and choice prior, trained on the same folds.
- Effect-size margins: EEG AUC − union AUC ≥ 0.03, and (EEG + union) − union ≥ 0.03, both with 95% CIs excluding 0. This replaces B's "partial information > 0", which is trivially passed at about 10^5 epochs.
- A topography test comparing the decoder's Haufe forward pattern with that person's measured ocular propagation map (20 blinks and 20 vertical saccades per session), requiring |ρ| < 0.5. This replaces B's circular "frontopolar peak = fail" rule.
- graft: Brain_reward should adopt generative's unannounced pixel-replay design and its rank statistic. The pre-registered statistic would be the rank of the privately held g_B among the K−1 goals other than g_A, with a dissatisfaction-only surrogate (frontal theta, corrugator RMS, blink rate, pupil) required to fail it. This replaces the partial correlation > 0 test, which is easy to pass at large n. Brain_reward should also extend the 2° fixation pause to teaching and robot blocks, as generative does. Without that, gaze toward the preferred location can leak into the frozen policy through the reward model.

- factual error: Checked and correct: both proposals' bits-per-evaluation tables for an equal-variance Gaussian decoder (AUC 0.60/0.65/0.70/0.75 gives 0.023/0.052/0.093/0.148 bits for a balanced label, and 0.039/0.071/0.113 at a 0.25 base rate). Brain_reward's 1,053/465/258/163 evaluations and 42/19/10/7 practical minutes for 24 bits are also correct. Note that brain_reward's teaching-time arithmetic treats every action as a binary r>0 vs r<0 event, while its label blocks include 2/8 zero-r actions (3:2:3), so realistic teaching times are somewhat longer.
- strongest objection to brain_reward: The proposal's main purity claim, that on ε-probe actions 'P(r | everything on screen) = P(r) ... includes the turn angle Δθ, cursor history ... by construction', is logically false. Probes are interleaved inside trials, after policy actions that the agent chose from a goal posterior built on goal-dependent EEG. So the visible trajectory, Δθ and the nearest ring all carry information about the goal, and r = cos(θ_probe − θ_goal) can be predicted from the display. The EEG can read it through cortical motion-adaptation and looming VEPs. The leak grows as the agent improves with hours of training, so the headline probe-AUC scaling curve, which has no matched display floor on probes and comes from a reward model retrained on all leaky epochs, could rise with data for purely display-driven reasons. It would repeat the RLNF failure the proposal says it avoids by construction.
- strongest objection to generative: The proposal's distinctive claim, that a generative world model of EEG turns unlabeled hours into goal information and so 'scales where discriminative stalled', cannot hold up as designed, because the design undermines it from two sides. First, its only real goal signal is the ErrP-type evaluation response. The proposal says so itself ('nearly all the information comes from the evaluation response'), and the project has already shown that this channel adds nothing measurable at AUC ≈0.68. To stay pure, the goal term is trained only on goal-blind random-cursor blocks. There, moves away from the goal are common and moves toward it are rare, the reverse of closed loop, where errors are rare. A prediction-error response learned in one regime is inverted in the other and can never be refit. Second, the scaling argument (background-variance shrinkage) is unsupported for scalp EEG. The proposal's own counter-evidence (foundation models give modest gains) and generative-vs-discriminative theory (Ng & Jordan 2001: generative wins at small n, loses asymptotically) both predict the opposite of its '+20–60% at ≥20 h' table. Strip those away and what remains is a known Bayesian ErrP goal-inference loop (Grizou 2014; Iturrate 2015 PLOS ONE) running at about 0.05–0.10 bits/s. Its purity rests on a replay statistic, P(g_B)/P(g_A), that frowning or non-specific dissatisfaction can pass, and the EMG auditor set leaves out the facial muscles (corrugator, frontalis) that would expose frowning.

## SF1: population vs generative — winner population (3–0)
- graft: Take B's unannounced frame-exact pixel replay and add it to A's closed loop. On 20-25% of trials, replay the visible-cursor trajectory from a past run aimed at a different ring while the participant holds a different pre-committed goal. The decoder's output must follow the private goal, not the replayed pixels. This directly tests the brief's "decoder reads the visual consequence of its own output" trap. A's current controls (hidden-cursor probes, sham feedback, the 45-degree rotation) do not close that gap for the visible-cursor segments. This is the only test proposed by either side that directly closes the brief's closed-loop trap, where a decoder learns to read the visual consequence of its own output. - strongest objection to smr_modern: smr_modern's case for purity rests on one small test: zero-shot held-out-mapping transfer (C2b). Each held-out mapping gets a single first-exposure block of about 16–30 trials, in three or four completers. The test is a ratio of near-floor margins on the x axis, which the proposal itself puts below floor for about 60% of users. When it fails, falsifier F1 cannot tell an impure decoder from a person who cannot remap 'imagine left hand to go up' on first try. Most of the training data, the closed-loop L2 regime, can be predicted exactly from gaze × the announced mapping M, and a CNN is placed there that can learn that interaction. C2b passes even if 30% of the margin is leak, and that leak stays fully active in every identity-mapping use, including the 'just intend' naturalness endpoint. So the design never shows 'natural' and 'pure' in the same condition. Its own data undercut its scaling claim: a population prior with zero own trials (0.736) is about equal to own data (0.741). Predicted throughput (0.05–0.3 bits/s) does not exceed what the project has already established, and the result is a validation control on a 2004 paradigm, not a new capability.
- strongest objection to vocabulary: Vocabulary concedes the brief's goal. It says it 'does not deliver dense continuous control', its steer mode is 'not claimed until re-simulated', and the robot works through goal-mode menus at a median of 0.04–0.1 bits/s (30–75 s per goal). Its scientific bet is that idiosyncratic cognitive tasks survive the purity gate, and the gate is built so they fail. Task-driven blink, pupil and gaze changes in mental arithmetic and word generation (its own citation, Loetscher 2010) will push the per-task auditor surrogate above the permutation p95 in test 1. Meanwhile the 4–20 Hz montage without F7/F8/T7/T8 removes the language and auditory sources. The proposal names this outcome (F2) as its biggest risk and says it 'reduces to SMR'. It would then be a slower, discrete, less natural version of the SMR continuous-control design it is competing against.
