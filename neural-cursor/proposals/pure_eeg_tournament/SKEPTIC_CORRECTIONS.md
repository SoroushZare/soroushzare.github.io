# Skeptic corrections to the champion's load-bearing claims (apply them; they override the proposal)

## Claims
- C1: Physiological premise: imagined foot movement versus both-hands movement gives a sustained, decodable mu/beta (8-30 Hz) ERD contrast over central channels (vertex vs C3/C4) that dry electrodes can pick up. On gel ds004362 the feet/fists task scores 0.700 (0.5-3.5 s, central 8-30 Hz) and peaks at 0.667 on 1-s windows at 1-2 s (0.629 at 2-3 s). E1 puts 65% of subjects above floor for imagined fists/feet, against 38% for imagined L/R.
- C2: Dry-cap penalty premise: dry electrodes cost only 20-40% of gel d-prime. The proposal admits this has no literature source. It gives dry per-1-s r on y of 0.25-0.33, x of 0.11-0.23, and session-1 feet-vs-hands accuracy of 0.60-0.68.
- C3: Display-confound premise: on ds004362, occipital channels decode the DISPLAYED target nearly as well as sensorimotor ones (E3: 0.604 L/R, 0.661 fists/feet vs 0.629/0.706 central). Posterior alpha regressed on central drops to 0.529/0.541. So labels a display can identify (ReFIT target-minus-cursor) let a decoder learn visual attention instead of motor intent. It follows that a decoder trained only on fixation-verified L1 probes and blind L2b closed loop (label-symmetric tone, cues never a location) carries no such leak, and a sighted-label shadow decoder will show a measurable beta_screen > 0.
- C4: Scaling premise for one person: decoder accuracy saturates quickly with own labelled data at fixed SNR. E2b: the population prior scores 0.736 with 0 own trials and 0.741 with 30. E6: own-from-scratch catches the prior by about 40 trials. The repo simulation saturates by day 2. Hence H1 predicts at most +0.03 r from 5 h to 60 h, while extra sessions buy robustness to dry-cap non-stationarity (H2, at least +0.05 r from 3 to 20 sessions, based on the simulated collapse of a frozen decoder under drift).
- C5: User-learning premise: SMR control improves through operant practice (Wolpaw & McFarland 2004; Perdikis 2018), including with blind or auditory feedback (Nijboer 2008, initially slower). L1 separability with a frozen decoder is predicted to rise by at least 0.05 accuracy on y by 30 h of dry use, and r on y to reach 0.30-0.45 at 30-60 h.
- C6: Feasibility premise: 30-45% of participants will earn a lateralised L/R x axis. Basis: E1, 38% above floor for imagined L/R on gel; on 1-s windows the top-third responders keep 0.729/0.691/0.656, but the population mean falls from 0.616 to 0.537. This is achievable within the protocol of 4 deep participants of about 60 h each plus 28 broad participants of 7.5 h each (about 450 h dry, 2 rigs, 1 postdoc, 2 RAs), with enough statistical power for the pooled P2 mixed model over 32 participants and for the per-session ladders.

## verify:skeptic1
- C1 (refuted=True, confidence 0.7): On gel ds004362, untrained imagined fists/feet is decodable (0.70 over 0.5-3.5 s; 61-65% of subjects above floor), but the 1-s signal peaks at 1-2 s (0.667) and decays (0.629, then 0.610), so it is transient, not sustained. Roughly a third of its margin is shared with occipital, display-driven activity. That dry electrodes capture it is an untested assumption.
- C2 (refuted=True, confidence 0.8): The dry penalty is an unsourced assumption. Small lab studies suggest near-parity in classification accuracy, but no study gives a long-term dry-SMR d' penalty. The gel anchors being scaled also include display-shared variance. All dry numbers in Section 4 should be presented as a sensitivity range, for example a 0-70% d' loss applied to a motor-only gel anchor, not as a 20-40% prediction.
- C3 (refuted=True, confidence 0.8): Offline gel data show the displayed-target label is substantially decodable from posterior and ocular channels. Part of central decoding is shared with occipital, and eye proxies out-decode motor channels for L/R across subjects (E7). Fixation-verified, non-spatial cues remove overt display reading. They do not by themselves exclude covert spatial attention or direction imagery tied to the body-part labels, so the leak claim needs an empirical test (for example posterior-alpha lateralisation audits on L1/L2b). The shadow decoder's detectable beta_screen is untested and needs a power estimate.
- C4 (refuted=True, confidence 0.8): Real data cover only 45 trials per person. Within that range own-only accuracy is still rising, and the pooled prior looks flat because own trials carry about 9% of the weight on a 15-trial test set. H1 (≤ +0.03 r from 5 h to 60 h) and H2 (≥ +0.05 r from session diversity) are untested hypotheses, not consequences of evidence. The Stieger 2021 analysis should be run before either is quoted as a prediction with a basis.
- C5 (refuted=True, confidence 0.7): SMR user learning is well documented for gel systems, mostly with co-adaptive decoders and few users (W&M 2004, n=4; Perdikis 2018, n=2), and with a fixed control signal (Meng & He 2019; Stieger 2021). Auditory feedback slows early learning (Nijboer 2008). Behavioural improvement does not always show up as separability at fixed channels (Meng & He 2019). The predictions of +0.05 L1 accuracy by 30 h and r 0.30-0.45 on dry are assumptions with no dry or blind precedent.
- C6 (refuted=True, confidence 0.75): Gel, display-visible data put roughly 33% (after removing chance exceedances) of untrained subjects above floor on imagined L/R. A sizeable part of that signal is shared with posterior and ocular channels, so 30-45% dry, blind earners is optimistic. A rate of 10-25% is plausible. With 4 deep participants there is a 24-41% chance that none earns x. F5 (fewer than 25% of 32 earning x) has a substantial prior probability of firing, and the P2 power analysis has to be done rather than asserted.

## verify:skeptic3
- C1 (refuted=True, confidence 0.6): Feet-vs-hands mu/beta ERD is a well-established physiological contrast, and it is decodable on gel ds004362 (0.700 at 0.5-3.5 s, 61-65% of subjects above floor). It peaks at 1-2 s and fades: 45% of subjects are above floor at 2-3 s (r about 0.31) and 40% at 3-4 s (r about 0.27). About half of the central margin is shared with posterior channels, so in this dataset it cannot be separated from top/bottom display or gaze effects. Whether a dry cap supports sustained y control is untested, not established.
- C2 (refuted=True, confidence 0.65): The 20-40% d' penalty is an unsourced planning guess. Short-session dry-vs-gel MI and P300 studies (Jeong 2021, Saab 2011, Guger 2012) suggest small losses in the lab, but nothing covers long daily dry wear. Under the proposal's own conversion, a 20-40% penalty gives sustained (2-3 s) per-1-s r on y of about 0.19-0.25, not 0.25-0.33, which uses the peak 1-2 s window. If the penalty is 50-70%, sustained y is r 0.10-0.16, and only 16-25% of subjects stay above floor on sustained windows. The dry-penalty pilot is therefore a go/no-go gate for the P1/P2 margins, not a calibration detail.
- C3 (refuted=True, confidence 0.7): In ds004362 the central and posterior 8-30 Hz information is largely shared: each set drops to near chance when regressed on the other, and central drops by about half its margin when posterior is regressed out. Display-driven attention and volume-conducted motor ERD cannot be told apart there, and the larger evidenced leak for L/R is ocular (eye proxy 0.72 zero-shot). Fixation-verified and blind labels remove display-identifiable labels but not cue-locked auditory or microsaccade leaks. Whether a central-montage sighted-label shadow shows a detectable beta_screen > 0 in closed loop is unknown. It has to be demonstrated in simulation and pilot before P2 can be called a certification test.
- C4 (refuted=True, confidence 0.75): The only real-data evidence stops at 45 trials per person and shows own-data accuracy still climbing (own-only 0.616 at 8 trials to 0.677 at 30; adding 45 own executed trials gives +0.066). E2b/E6 cannot resolve gains below about 0.06. The 'at most +0.03 r from 5 h to 60 h' prediction (H1) is a hypothesis with no empirical support, and it is less plausible for the EEGNet branch. H2 (at least +0.05 r from 3 to 20 sessions) rests on simulated drift only. Stieger 2021 (598 sessions, figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.14153504) should be analysed before either is pre-registered.
- C5 (refuted=True, confidence 0.6): SMR operant learning is documented with gel and sighted (or direction-informative auditory) feedback, in small samples (n=2-16 for long training) and over 3 sessions in 42 users. In the 42-user study the gains appeared in online accuracy more than in C3/C4 separability. No evidence covers learning on a dry cap under label-symmetric blind feedback. H3 (at least +0.05 accuracy on y by 30 h) is a plausible but unsupported hypothesis. The r 0.30-0.45 target at 30-60 h is optimistic: from a sustained dry start of about 0.19-0.25 it requires gains with no precedent.
- C6 (refuted=True, confidence 0.7): On gel with the target visible, about 27% of subjects show true sustained (2-3 s) lateralised L/R decoding (raw 33% minus chance), and part of it is ocular or posterior-shared. Under the proposal's 20-40% dry penalty the expected earner fraction is about 17-22%. That is below the F5 and kill-rule threshold of 25%, so the most likely outcome is a 1-D headline, and the 30-45% figure holds only for 3-s windows under the optimistic penalty. The 450 h, 2-rig plan is logistically feasible. The within-person H1-H3 decomposition, however, rests on n=4 deep participants and is underpowered for population claims. The pooled P2 test is driven mostly by those 4 people, plus only the few x-earners on the x axis.
