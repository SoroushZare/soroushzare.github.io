# Neural cursor — design of record

Continuous 2-D cursor control from a g.tec 32-channel dry hybrid-electrode cap at 500 Hz, with no
external stimulation and no instructed motor imagery, designed to improve with data.

Companions: `proposals/` (the seven competing designs and the judge's `VERDICT.md`),
`SYNAPTRIX_ANALYSIS.md`, `neural_cursor/` (reference implementation), `results/` (simulation outputs,
regenerated by the commands in §7), `tests/`.

---

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

Seven paradigms competed under one rubric (naturalness 25, achievable performance 20, scaling with
data 20, validity 15, feasibility 10, rigour 10). The judge ranked them G 6.65 > E 5.85 > F 5.50 >
D 5.40 > A 5.20 > C 5.15 > B 5.00: the stimulus-driven paradigms (SSVEP, P300) and motor imagery fail
the user's own constraints; the purely cortical stimulus-free signals (covert attention, error
responses, low-frequency "kinematics") carry 0.03–0.3 bits/s on this cap; and the only natural,
stimulus-free cursor above 0.5 bits/s from these electrodes is one where the frontal channels read
the eyes. The design of record is therefore a **two-track composite**: a shared-autonomy controller
(an agent that infers the goal from a posterior over on-screen targets and never reads it) driven by
every stimulus-free signal the cap carries, with the ocular and cortical contributions **separated by
design and reported as measured increments**. Simulated end to end: a weak cortical velocity decoder
(r ≈ 0.4) reaches 8 targets at 0.49 success alone and 0.78–0.83 with the goal posterior, while the
EOG-only and no-neural surrogates stay at 0.00–0.01; an error-evidence channel at AUC ≈ 0.68 adds
nothing over that posterior, and a frozen decoder collapses under dry-electrode gain drift unless
re-centred (§3, §4). The honest headline for the real cap is
0.1–0.3 bits/s cortical-only with assistance, 0.8–1.5 bits/s when the eyes are allowed to point; the
protocol in §6 is built to say exactly which of the two a given result is.

## 1. The question and its constraints

| item | value |
|---|---|
| hardware | g.tec 32-ch dry hybrid cap, 10-20 layout incl. FP1/FP2, 500 Hz, LSL; no EOG pair, no eye tracker (one is added by this design) |
| task | continuous 2-D cursor movement and selection on a screen |
| user constraints | no flicker/oddball stimulation, no instructed hand/foot imagery; "just naturally control the cursor" |
| framing | bitter lesson: general learning + data volume; a data engine where ordinary use yields labels; co-adaptation |
| prior evidence | RLNF (29 participants, 11.5k trials): EEG-gated accept/reject loop; prospective AUC 0.66–0.69 against a label that was a threshold on the displayed angle; nothing improved the decoder; ocular confound unidentifiable; no task benefit because a rejection cost 5.25 s |

Rules carried over from RLNF, now design constraints rather than post-hoc lessons: labels never a
threshold on a displayed variable; a non-neural surrogate matched to every decoder; permutation
floors at matched n; fixed test sets across resource ladders; no performance-based exclusions;
per-channel amplitude gating with impedance logging; controller utility as the primary endpoint;
estimability audit before recording.

## 2. The competition

Seven agents each wrote a full proposal to the same brief (`proposals/00_BRIEF.md`); an independent
judge re-derived every score and checked the citations (`proposals/VERDICT.md`).

| proposal | signal | natural | perf | scaling | validity | feasib | rigour | weighted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A SSVEP / c-VEP | flicker-evoked occipital | 3 | 6 | 4 | 7 | 6 | 8 | 5.20 |
| B P300 | oddball ERP | 2 | 5 | 4 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 5.00 |
| C motor imagery / SMR | mu/beta ERD | 3 | 5 | 6 | 6 | 5 | 8 | 5.15 |
| D covert attention | alpha lateralisation | 6 | 2 | 5 | 8 | 5 | 8 | 5.40 |
| E shared autonomy + ErrP | error response to agent moves | 6 | 3 | 6 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 5.85 |
| F end-to-end scaled | mouse-labelled low-frequency EEG | 4 | 3 | 7 | 8 | 5 | 8 | 5.50 |
| G EOG hybrid | cap-channel gaze + EEG click | 7 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 7 | 8 | **6.65** |

What the judge found, in short:
- **A, B** are the fastest decoders in the field (SSVEP 0.3–0.7 bits/s realistic on this montage;
  P300 0.2–0.3 bits/s) and fail the no-stimulation constraint by construction. Both carry a gaze
  dependence that only an eye tracker can audit.
- **C** needs 5 instructed-imagery sessions before the instruction can be withdrawn, 15–30 % of users
  never gain control, and the realistic dry figure is 0.15–0.4 bits/s.
- **D** is the most natural cortical signal but delivers ≈0.05 bits/s; the user must *not* look at the
  target, which is the opposite of natural pointing.
- **E** has the best label structure (labels come from the hidden goal and the logged action) and the
  best scaling story (every hour of use yields ~2,500 labels plus a sharper goal prior), but at
  0.12 bits/decision the person evaluates rather than steers: 20–35 s per target.
- **F** has the best identifiability design and a mechanistic scaling curve r(h) saturating near
  r ≈ 0.35, but its natural (hand-still, eyes-fixed) regime is the weakest signal of all.
- **G** wins the rubric because it meets all four constraints and reaches usable throughput; it is
  not brain control, and says so. A consumer eye tracker beats its backbone 2–3×.
- Citation errors were found in A, B, D, F, G (venues, subject counts, periorbital vs frontal EOG
  gains); none changes the ranking.

**The decision the user has to make**, stated by the judge and endorsed here: "neural cursor" can
mean *cortex* (0.1–0.3 bits/s with assistance on this cap, and a scientific result either way) or
*no stimulation, no imagery, from this hardware* (≈1 bit/s, mostly ocular). The design of record
serves both by measuring them separately on the same trials.

## 3. Round 1 — the composite controller, simulated end to end

The paradigm-independent system (acquisition, causal filtering, amplitude gating, features, decoders,
cursor task, shared-autonomy posterior, logging, evaluation) was built first; a deterministic
simulated user drives it. The simulator embeds weak, calibrated signals (alpha lateralisation for
horizontal intent, central beta desynchronisation for vertical intent, an optional signed frontal
gaze potential, an optional evoked frontocentral error response) in realistic 1/f noise. It is a
regression harness, not a model of the brain: it tests that the *system* turns a weak decoder into
control and that the *controls* catch what they must catch.

Setup: 8 targets on a ring, ISO 9241-9 conditions (distance 0.5/0.8 screen, width 0.08/0.05),
dwell 0.8 s, timeout 15 s, 6 simulated users × 24 trials, identical blocks and seeds per condition.
Floors are the null's 95th percentile at matched n. Commands in §7.

| features | gaze leak | decoder r | EOG-only surrogate r |
|---|---|---|---|

A time-domain low-frequency feature (the "kinematics from EEG" family, proposal F's raw regime)
*improves* the decoder when gaze leaks, and the EOG-only surrogate flags it. Band power on
≥ 1 Hz discards the sign of a slow gaze potential and is largely immune. This is the RLNF trap
reproduced and detected in the harness.

## 4. Round 2 — thinking as a neuroscientist, then redesigning

### 4.1 What the field has established, signal by signal

| signal | best replicated evidence | what it buys here | main confound | dry-electrode effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parieto-occipital alpha lateralisation (covert attention) | Worden 2000, Thut 2006, Kelly 2005; BCI: van Gerven & Jensen 2009, Treder 2011, Tonin 2013: ~70 % L/R per 2 s | a weak continuous left/right prior; vertical ≈ chance | microsaccades toward the attended side (Yuval-Greenberg 2008) | alpha is 8–13 Hz and large: survives dry pins if occipital contact is good |
| error-related response to a machine's action | Ferrez & Millán 2008; Chavarriaga 2010/2014; Iturrate 2015; Lopes-Dias 2019 (asynchronous): single-trial 0.65–0.85 gel | a discrete 0.07–0.28 bits per agent segment | visual motion-onset ERP when the agent turns (judge's turn-angle point); saccade to the "error" | 1–10 Hz, 5–10 µV: feasible but the first thing drift hurts; expect −0.05 |
| low-frequency "kinematics" | Bradberry 2010 r 0.2–0.4, reduced to ≈0.2–0.3 after ocular correction (Antelis 2013, Kobler 2018, Mondini 2020, Pulferer 2022) | a direction prior during overt movement; much less when the hand and eyes are still | dominated by EOG unless gaze is measured and regressed | DC/0.1 Hz drift on dry pins is worst exactly in this band |
| sensorimotor rhythms (attempted/imagined movement) | Wolpaw & McFarland 2004; Edelman 2019; Stieger 2021: 0.2–0.6 bits/s after many sessions | the only signal with a demonstrated 2-D continuous record | EMG, and requires instruction | large, mid-band: survives dry pins |
| ocular potentials on frontal channels | 5–10 µV/deg at frontal cap sites; Belkacem 2015 ≈ 90 % of 4–8 directions from cap sites | pointing at ≈ 0.5–1.2 bits/s | it *is* the eye | DC drift; needs a tracker or EOG pair as anchor |

Three facts organise the redesign. (i) Every stimulus-free cortical signal on this cap is weak:
r ≈ 0.2–0.4 or AUC ≈ 0.6–0.75 per second of data. (ii) Information from weak evidence compounds only
if something *integrates* it: a goal posterior does, and it does so without reading the goal.
(iii) The dominant confound is the same for all of them, the eyes, so one instrument (an eye tracker
recorded but not used for control) audits the whole system.

### 4.2 Pros, cons and fallbacks of the round-1 composite

| component | pro | con | fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| continuous velocity decoder on band power | stimulus-free; immune to signed gaze leak; cheap | weak (r 0.2–0.4); vertical worse than horizontal | treat it as a prior into the posterior, never the sole driver |
| goal posterior (shared autonomy) | doubles usable control (0.49 → 0.78); never reads the goal | Midas touch if the agent commits on a flat posterior (round-2 bug found in simulation: success fell to 0.22 when the agent dwelled on its guess) | commit only above a posterior threshold; hover outside the target otherwise; ε-exploration keeps every label estimable |
| error-evidence channel after agent segments | best-replicated endogenous signal; labels from the hidden goal | gradual errors evoke smaller, later responses (Omedes 2015); turn-onset visual ERP can masquerade; needs ≥ 400 labels before it clears its floor (ladder below) | keep as an audited increment; discrete salient segment onsets; display-only surrogate *with turn angle* |
| frontal-channel gaze (EOG) | the only > 0.5 bits/s stimulus-free pointer on this cap | not brain; dry DC drift | report it as its own arm; anchor with the eye tracker; never pool with the cortical arm |
| dry electrodes | no gel, fast setup | drift, motion artefacts, EMG sensitivity | 0.5 Hz causal high-pass, per-window amplitude gate (5× session median RMS, FP1/FP2 exempt), online re-centring, impedance log |
| co-adaptation / data engine | every completed trial reveals the goal → every segment gets a label with no extra task | non-stationarity across sessions; RLNF saw no session-level adaptation | nightly retraining with a frozen test session; resource ladder with the test set fixed |

### 4.3 The redesigned system (design of record)

```
                 ┌──────────────── eye tracker (recorded, never used for control in the cortical arm)
                 │
EEG 32ch 500Hz ──┼─► causal HP 0.5 / LP 40 / notch ─► CAR over gated channels ─► amplitude gate
                 │        │                                                         │
                 │        ├─► 0.5 s windows, 10 Hz ─► log band power ─► ridge ─► velocity v̂ (cortical)
                 │        ├─► FP1/FP2/F7/F8 ─► gaze model ─► position ĝ (ocular arm, separate)
                 │        └─► 0.2–0.8 s after each agent segment ─► ERP time course ─► logistic ─► p(accept)
                 │
                 ▼
     goal posterior over on-screen targets  ◄── v̂ (von Mises likelihood)  ◄── p(accept) (segment evidence)
                 │
                 ▼
     agent: MAP target with ε = 0.2 exploration; hover at 0.14 until p_max ≥ 0.8; proportional approach
                 │
                 ▼
     cursor = (1 − a)·v̂ + a·agent,  a ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} (pre-registered arms)  ─► dwell 0.8 s selects
                 │
                 ▼
     trial ends → goal revealed → every window and segment of the trial is labelled → data engine
```

Changes from round 1, each traceable to a finding above: confidence-gated commit and hover (Midas
touch); ε-exploration (positivity); discrete 1 s agent segments with explicit onsets (ErrP salience,
cheap rejection: a wrong segment costs 1 s, not 5.25 s); evidence decoder on the ERP time course, not
the covariance (a phase-locked transient lives in the time course: AUC 0.72 vs 0.60 in the harness);
400+ labelled segments before the evidence channel is switched on (ladder); eye tracker mandatory;
ocular arm reported separately; display-only surrogate with turn angle.

### 4.4 Round-2 simulation

The composite was re-simulated with the error-evidence channel in two forms, a dry-penalised evoked
response, a weaker velocity signal and a 10 %/min gain drift. Conditions:

- **decoder + posterior**: the round-1 reference (continuous posterior blend, a = 0.5).
- **assist + evidence**: the same, plus an evidence packet every second about the last second's net
  cursor motion (the person watched the cursor go right or wrong); **assist + shuffled**: its surrogate.
- **agent + evidence**: velocity decoder off; the agent steers in 1 s segments from the posterior
  with ε = 0.2 exploration and the hover/commit gate; evidence about each segment.
- **composite** / **composite shuffled**: velocity decoder + segment agent + evidence, and its surrogate.
- **agent only**, **random**: the agent's own floor and the task's floor.

Two bugs were found by simulation before any human was recorded, and both are the kind of thing
RLNF paid for in participants: an agent that commits on a flat posterior dwell-selects wrong targets
(success 0.22 with the evidence channel on), and an agent that pushes the cursor *back out* of a
target it is not yet confident about fights the person (success 0.24). Both are fixed in the code of
record (hover without pushing; commit only above p 0.8).

The finding of record from this round: **at evidence AUC ≈ 0.68 and one packet per second, the
error-evidence channel does not add measurable control over the dense velocity posterior** (ten
packets per second from a decoder at r ≈ 0.4), and the segmented agent is a worse assistance scheme
than the continuous blend because exploration and hovering cost motion. The design of record
therefore uses the continuous posterior blend as the controller and keeps the error-evidence
channel as a pre-registered optional arm that must beat its shuffled surrogate before it is
enabled; the simulation predicts it will not at dry-cap accuracies, and the judge's "no ErrP veto"
stands. Tables below are generated into `results/sim_round2_*.md` by the commands in §7.

## 5. What the system is, precisely

**Signals used for control (cortical arm).** Log band power in 1–4, 4–8, 8–13, 13–20, 20–30 Hz on
32 CAR channels over 0.5 s windows every 0.1 s → ridge regression (α = 100, inner-CV) to a 2-D
command; ERP time course (0.2–0.8 s after a segment onset, block-averaged to 25 Hz, 32 × 15 features)
→ L2 logistic (C = 0.01) → p(accept). Both are linear so every weight can be audited against a
covariance-matched random-filter null.

**Signals used for control (ocular arm, reported separately).** FP1/FP2 (vertical) and F8 − F7
(horizontal) → ridge to gaze position, taught by the eye tracker, Kalman-smoothed; blink freeze.

**Controller.** Goal posterior over the K selectable targets (von Mises likelihood on the decoded
direction, κ = |v̂|; Bernoulli likelihood on segment evidence); agent as in §4.3; cursor velocity
control with exponential smoothing 0.7 per 100 ms step, max speed 0.6 screen/s, dwell 0.8 s.

**Data engine.** Every completed trial reveals the goal, so every 0.5 s window gets a direction label
and every agent segment gets an accept/veto label (did the logged move reduce the distance to the
revealed goal). Nightly retraining; a frozen test session per participant; a resource ladder with
that test set fixed. Cross-participant pretraining (pooled tangent-space / ridge priors, later a
pretrained EEG encoder such as LaBraM/CBraMod fine-tuned to the same heads) is a pre-registered arm,
expected to buy a faster start, not a higher asymptote.

**Identifiability by design.** The label is a function of the hidden goal and the logged action,
never of a displayed threshold; the agent explores with ε = 0.2 so every target has positive
probability under every posterior; the eye tracker is recorded in every block; fixation-verified
epochs (< 1.5°) define the cortical test set; surrogates run on the same trials: EOG-only decoder,
shuffled-evidence composite, posterior-alone, display-only model with turn angle, phase-scrambled EEG.

## 6. Data-collection protocol

**Participants.** 24, no performance-based exclusion, 4 held out of every model choice. Power: the
primary utility contrast (composite vs shuffled-evidence composite, paired within participant) at
d = 0.6 needs n = 24 for 0.80; the decoder-above-floor contrast at RLNF's between-subject SD (0.07)
is powered > 0.95.

**Sessions.** 6 × 60 min per participant on separate days; session 6 is the frozen test session and
informs no choice. Two participants continue for 10 further sessions (scaling arm).

**Session structure (minutes).**

| block | min | purpose | label |
|---|---|---|---|
| setup, impedance log, 60 s eyes-open/closed, amplitude gate baseline | 8 | gating, alpha peak | — |
| eye-tracker calibration + 2 min free viewing | 4 | ocular model teacher | tracker |
| cued open-loop directions (random cue, 0.5 s hold, 200 cues) | 5 | velocity decoder labels independent of EEG | cue |
| random-agent block (goal cued, agent moves at random for 1 s segments, 240 segments) | 6 | evidence decoder labels | goal × move |
| closed-loop Fitts blocks, 4 arms a ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, Latin-square, 8 targets, ISO 9241-9 distances/widths, 15 s timeout | 24 | primary endpoints | revealed goal |
| fixation-verified block (tracker-enforced, cursor-locked gaze) | 5 | cortical test set | revealed goal |
| free-use block (menus/grid with a Zipf target distribution) | 6 | goal-prior learning, data engine | revealed goal |
| delayed keypress ratings on 20 % of segments ("was that move toward your target?") | within blocks | label-noise audit | keypress |

**Logged over LSL with one clock.** Raw EEG (µV, pre-filter), eye tracker (gaze, pupil, validity),
markers (segment onset, agent target, posterior vector, command, outcome, gate flags, impedance at
block start), cursor state every control step, config hash, model hash. Nothing derived from the
decoder is ever a label.

**Pre-registration list.** Endpoints (§8), arms, floors, surrogates, gating thresholds, the r(h) and
AUC(n) ladder predictions, held-out participants, hyperparameters (`neural_cursor/config.py`),
code hash, the falsifiers of §8.

## 7. Implementation

Live rig: replace `SimulatedEEG` with `LSLEEG` (same interface), add a renderer that subscribes to
`TrialRunner.run(..., on_step=...)`, and feed the eye tracker as a second LSL inlet. The decoders,
posterior, agent, logger and evaluation are unchanged.

**Hyperparameters of record** (all in `config.py`): HP 0.5 Hz, LP 40 Hz, notch 50 Hz, Butterworth
order 4, CAR; gate 5× session median RMS, FP1/FP2 exempt; window 0.5 s, hop 0.1 s; bands as above;
ridge α 100; ERP features at 25 Hz, logistic C 0.01; re-centring buffer 64, prior 5; control 10 Hz,
max speed 0.6, smoothing 0.7, target radius 0.08, dwell 0.8 s, timeout 15 s, K = 8; agent ε 0.2,
segment 1 s, commit p ≥ 0.8, hover 0.14; permutations 2,000, bootstrap 2,000, seed 20261004.

**Latency budget per control step (10 Hz).** Filter on a 50-sample chunk < 1 ms; Welch on 250 × 32
≈ 2 ms; ridge < 0.1 ms; posterior update < 0.1 ms; the amplifier + LSL path (≈ 40 ms, to be measured
and logged) dominates. The evidence epoch is scored once per second.

## 8. Evaluation protocol

**Primary endpoints (co-primary, pre-registered).**
- E1 Controller utility: Fitts throughput (ISO 9241-9 effective, successful trials), time-to-target,
  success at 15 s, for each arm, on the frozen test session; the paired contrasts composite vs
  shuffled-evidence composite and composite vs posterior-alone.
- E2 Cortical increment: on fixation-verified epochs, (EEG-only + posterior) minus (ocular-only +
  posterior) throughput, with a TOST bound of ± 0.05 bits/s.

**Secondary.** Velocity r and evidence AUC against permutation floors at matched n (the null's 95th
percentile, stated as such), per participant with a prediction interval for a new participant;
posterior calibration (Brier); label-noise rate from the keypress audit; the r(h) and AUC(n) ladders
with the test set fixed; drift of the operating point within a session.

**Surrogates on the same trials.** EOG-only decoder; shuffled-evidence composite; posterior alone;
display-only model (direction, position, time, posterior, turn angle); phase-scrambled EEG; a
covariance-matched random-filter null for every fitted spatial pattern.

**Statistics.** Mixed models with participant intercepts; exact paired sign-flip tests with Holm over
the pre-registered family; effect sizes with CIs beside every p; equivalence by TOST where a null is
claimed; nothing from the test session informs a choice.

**Falsifiers.**
1. E2 TOST-equivalent to zero and EEG-only velocity r ≤ circular-shift floor + 0.05 on the test
   session → the cortical term is removed and the product is reported as "eye tracker + shared
   autonomy".
2. Composite ≤ shuffled-evidence composite on E1 → the evidence channel is removed.
3. Display-only model with turn angle ≥ evidence AUC − 0.02 → the "error response" is a visual-onset
   response; the channel is renamed and its weight frozen.
4. AUC(640) ≤ AUC(80) + 0.03 on the ladder → no scaling with data; the data-engine claim is dropped.
5. Ocular arm < 0.6 bits/s on the test session → the cap-EOG backbone is replaced by the tracker.

## 9. Failure modes and debugging

| failure | symptom | check | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| gaze leak into the cortical arm | EOG-only surrogate above its floor; decoder improves when slow features are added | the §3 feature comparison on real data; tracker–decoder correlation on LOOK-STILL epochs | band-power features only; gaze regression; fixation-verified test set |
| Midas touch | wrong targets selected fast | agent commits below p 0.8 | hover/commit gate; raise dwell |
| posterior self-confirmation | success high in all arms incl. posterior-alone | posterior-alone surrogate above random | the posterior must be updated only from decoder outputs, never from the blended command |
| evidence channel at floor | AUC ≈ 0.5–0.6 with < 400 labels | ladder | collect more labels before enabling; salient segment onsets |
| dry drift | rising reject rate of the amplitude gate; operating point shifts | gate log; re-centring reference distance | 0.5 Hz HP; re-centring; re-seat pins; impedance log |
| EMG | broadband power in 20–30 Hz on temporal channels tracks the command | temporal-channel surrogate; jaw/neck still block | exclude T7/T8 from features; gate |
| non-stationarity across days | test-session drop | per-session short recalibration from the engine's own labels | pretrain + re-centre + 5 min cued block |
| label noise in free use | keypress disagreement > 15 % | keypress audit | ReFIT-style relabelling toward the acquired target |

## 11. Roadmap

1. Week 1–2: live rig on LSL with the simulator swapped for the cap; measure and log the amplifier
   latency; eye tracker inlet; renderer; dry-run the full session script with one pilot (not counted).
2. Week 3–4: estimability audit on the recorded pilot (positivity, collinearity, display mediation,
   generator-determined sequences); freeze config, code hash and pre-registration.
3. Week 5–12: 24 participants × 6 sessions; nightly retraining; weekly surrogate dashboard.
4. Week 13–16: frozen test sessions; ladders; the two long-haul participants continue.
5. Report both arms with their surrogates; the paper's claim is whichever arm survives its falsifier.
